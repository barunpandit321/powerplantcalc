Understanding Industrial Steam Tables (IAPWS-IF97)

Steam tables are fundamental reference tables that tabulate the physical and thermodynamic properties of water (liquid) and steam (vapor) across a range of pressures and temperatures. Modern steam engineering calculations are based on the IAPWS-IF97 formulation (International Association for the Properties of Water and Steam), adopted by ASME, ISO, and power engineering societies worldwide.

01

Saturation Temperature (Tsat) & Pressure (Psat)

Saturation defines the thermodynamic state where liquid water and vapor coexist in equilibrium. At any given pressure (Psat), water boils at an exact saturation temperature (Tsat). In a steam boiler drum, water and steam remain at saturation condition.

02

Specific Enthalpy (hf, hfg, hg)

hf is the sensible heat of saturated liquid. hfg is the latent heat of vaporization required to convert 1 kg of boiling water into dry saturated steam at constant pressure without changing temperature. hg is the total enthalpy of saturated vapor (hg = hf + hfg).

03

Specific Volume (vf & vg)

vf is the volume occupied by 1 kg of saturated water (≈ 0.001 m³/kg). vg is the specific volume of saturated vapor. Because steam expands over 1,600 times compared to water at atmospheric pressure, specific volume governs pipe sizing and valve selection.

04

Specific Entropy (sf & sg)

Entropy measures the degree of irreversibility and energy dispersion in thermodynamic systems. It serves as the baseline for evaluating isentropic expansion work in steam turbines (Wideal = h1 - h2s).

♨️

Wet Steam & Two-Phase Mixture Equations

In two-phase saturated mixtures (such as inside boiler steam drums, separator vessels, and LP steam turbine exhausts), physical properties depend directly on the Dryness Fraction (Vapor Quality, x):

Wet Steam Enthalpy (hx)
hx = hf + x · hfg
= (1 - x) · hf + x · hg
Calculates total heat content of the two-phase mixture (kJ/kg or Btu/lb).
Wet Steam Specific Volume (vx)
vx = vf + x · (vg - vf)
≈ x · vg   (since vg ≫ vf)
Calculates volume occupied per unit mass (m³/kg or ft³/lb).
Wet Steam Entropy (sx)
sx = sf + x · sfg
= sf + x · (hfg / Tsat)
Thermodynamic baseline for evaluating isentropic turbine expansion efficiency.
Vapor Quality / Dryness Fraction (x)
x = (h - hf) / hfg
= (v - vf) / (vg - vf)
Mass fraction of dry saturated vapor: x = 0 (100% boiling liquid), x = 1 (100% dry vapor).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between Saturated by Pressure and Saturated by Temperature tables?

Both tables represent the exact same thermodynamic saturation curve. The Pressure Table is indexed by round increments of pressure (e.g., 1 bar, 2 bar, 10 bar), which is ideal for plant operators monitoring steam headers and boiler gauges. The Temperature Table is indexed by round increments of temperature (e.g., 50°C, 100°C, 150°C), which is ideal when analyzing condenser cooling water or heat exchanger pinch points.

Why does latent heat (hfg) decrease as pressure increases?

At higher pressures, water molecules are packed closer together in the liquid phase, requiring less additional energy to overcome intermolecular bonds and expand into vapor. At the critical point (220.64 bar / 373.95°C), the latent heat hfg becomes exactly zero, and the boundary between liquid and vapor disappears completely.

What is gauge pressure vs absolute pressure in steam tables?

Physical thermodynamic equations strictly require Absolute Pressure (psia or bar a). Pressure gauges in power plants read Gauge Pressure (psig or bar g), which is the pressure above local atmospheric pressure (Pabs = Pgauge + 1.01325 bar or Pabs = Pgauge + 14.696 psi). Our steam table includes a convenient "Gauge Press" toggle to switch columns automatically.

How does IAPWS-IF97 compare to older IFC-67 steam tables?

IAPWS-IF97 was developed in 1997 to replace the 1967 IFC formulation. It provides vastly improved computational speed (over 50 times faster for turbine simulation routines), continuous derivatives across region boundaries, and exact consistency with the modern International Temperature Scale of 1990 (ITS-90).
