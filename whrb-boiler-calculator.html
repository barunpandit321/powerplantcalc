Waste Heat Recovery Boiler (WHRB / HRSG) Engineering Fundamentals

A Waste Heat Recovery Boiler (WHRB)—also designated as a Heat Recovery Steam Generator (HRSG) in gas turbine combined cycle facilities—captures sensible thermal energy from industrial exhaust gases to generate high-pressure superheated or saturated process steam without firing supplemental fuel.

🔥

Fundamental WHRB Thermodynamic Energy Balance

The thermal energy recovered from the exhaust gas stream and the maximum steam generation rate are governed by ASME PTC 4.4 and the Evaporator Pinch Point constraint:

Gross Heat Recovered (Q̇recovered)
Q̇recovered = ṁgas · C̄p,gas · (Tgas,in − Tgas,out) · (1 − ηcasing)
Total enthalpy extracted from flue gas across all boiler heat exchanger modules (kW or MW).
Steam Generation Capacity (ṁsteam)
ṁsteam = [ṁgas · C̄p,gas · (Tgas,in − (Tsat + ΔTpinch)) · (1 − ηcasing)] / [(hsteam − hecon,out) + B · (hf − hecon,out)]
Thermodynamic limit derived from the Evaporator Pinch Point energy balance (kg/s or t/h).

📋 Engineering Variable Definitions & Units

Symbol Parameter Name Standard Unit Description & Design Range
ṁgas Flue Gas Mass Flow Rate kg/s or t/h (lb/hr) Total mass flow rate of hot exhaust gas entering the WHRB
C̄p,gas Mean Isobaric Heat Capacity kJ/(kg·K) (Btu/(lb·°F)) Gas specific heat capacity (≈ 1.05 – 1.15 kJ/(kg·K) based on flue composition)

ΔTpinch Evaporator Pinch Point °C or °F Minimum temperature difference: Tgas,evap,out − Tsat (typically 10 – 20°C)
ΔTapproach Economizer Approach Point °C or °F Feedwater subcooling margin: Tsat − Tecon,out (typically 5 – 15°C)

B Blowdown Fraction fraction (0.01 – 0.03) Continuous boiler drum blowdown to control total dissolved solids (TDS)
01

The Pinch Point Constraint (ΔTpinch)

The Pinch Point is the minimum temperature difference between the cooled flue gas leaving the evaporator tube bank and the saturated water boiling inside the evaporator at Tsat. Selecting a smaller pinch point yields more steam generation but demands exponentially larger heat transfer surface area.

02

The Approach Point Margin (ΔTapproach)

The Approach Point represents the degree of subcooling of water exiting the economizer relative to the drum saturation temperature (Tecon,out = Tsat − ΔTapproach). A positive margin (5 – 15°C) ensures steam does not generate inside the economizer tubes, preventing water hammer and vapor locking.

03

Acid Dew Point & Stack Corrosion

In fuels containing sulfur (heavy oil, sour gas, or high-sulfur coal), flue gas exit temperature must remain above the sulfuric acid dew point (130 – 150°C) to prevent acid condensation on economizer tubes and chimney walls.

Authoritative Standards & Engineering Codes

  • ASME PTC 4.4 — Performance Test Code on Gas Turbine Heat Recovery Steam Generators. American Society of Mechanical Engineers.
  • IAPWS-IF97 — International Association for the Properties of Water and Steam Formulation 1997 for Industrial Use.
  • EN 12952 — Water-tube boilers and auxiliary installations - Heat recovery steam generators.
  • API 560 / ISO 13705 — Fired Heaters and Waste Heat Recovery in Petroleum and Petrochemical Industries.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between a WHRB and an HRSG?

In thermal engineering practice, both terms refer to waste heat recovery boilers. However, the designation HRSG (Heat Recovery Steam Generator) is predominantly used in combined-cycle power plants downstream of a combustion gas turbine. WHRB (Waste Heat Recovery Boiler) is more commonly used in heavy industrial processes such as cement rotary kilns, steel sponge iron/DRI kilns, incinerators, and chemical reactors.

Why is the Pinch Point critical in WHRB design?

The pinch point defines the thermodynamic bottleneck of heat transfer. Because boiling takes place at a constant temperature (Tsat), the flue gas temperature cannot drop below the saturation temperature. The temperature difference at the exit of the evaporator (Tgas,evap − Tsat) governs the maximum heat that can be absorbed by the evaporator and superheater combined.

How does drum pressure affect steam generation?

Higher drum pressure raises the saturation temperature (Tsat) according to IAPWS-IF97 steam tables (e.g., Tsat = 180°C at 10 bar, but 253°C at 42 bar). A higher Tsat increases the evaporator exit gas temperature, which reduces the amount of sensible heat available above the pinch point, resulting in a lower steam generation rate for the same exhaust gas inlet temperature.

Can a WHRB operate on natural circulation or does it need a circulation pump?

Horizontal gas flow WHRBs with vertical evaporator tubes typically employ natural circulation, relying on the density difference between cold water in the external downcomers and the boiling two-phase mixture in the riser tubes. Vertical gas flow boilers with horizontal tube bundles typically require forced circulation pumps (BCPs) to ensure adequate boiling velocity and tube wall cooling.
