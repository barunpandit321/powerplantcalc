Input Hot Water Inlet Temp (Thot), Cold Water Outlet Temp (Tcold), and Ambient Wet-Bulb Temp (Twb).

2. Operational Engineering Definitions

Metric Symbol Engineering Application in Power Plants
Cooling Tower Range ΔT The temperature difference between hot entering water and cold leaving water. Higher Range indicates higher thermal load removed from steam turbine condensers.
Approach Temperature Approach The difference between cold leaving water and ambient wet-bulb temperature. Indicates cooling tower thermal adequacy (standard industrial approach is 3°C to 5°C).
Cooling Effectiveness η (%) Percentage ratio of actual cooling range achieved versus theoretical maximum potential cooling.
Cycles of Concentration CoC Ratio of dissolved mineral concentration in circulating basin water compared to raw makeup water. Higher CoC saves makeup water.

3. Water Loss & Performance Calculation Formulas

Understanding the mathematical derivations helps plant engineers optimize blowdown and chemical dosing:

Cooling Effectiveness (%) Formula

η =
Thot - Tcold Thot - Twb
× 100%

Evaporation Loss (E) Formula

E = 0.00085 × Q × (Thot - Tcold)

Approximately 0.15% of circulating flow evaporates per 1°C of cooling range achieved.

Blowdown Rate (B) Formula

B =
Evaporation Loss (E) CoC - 1

Blowdown prevents scale accumulation by bleeding off concentrated dissolved minerals.

Total Makeup Water Demand (M)

M = Evaporation (E) + Blowdown (B) + Drift Loss

Maintains constant water level in the cooling tower cold water basin.

4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Can a cooling tower cool water below ambient wet-bulb temperature?

No. Ambient wet-bulb temperature (Twb) is the theoretical thermodynamic limit for evaporative cooling. A cooling tower can never cool water below Twb.

Q: Why is increasing Cycles of Concentration (CoC) important?

Increasing CoC from 2.0 to 4.0 or 5.0 drastically reduces blowdown discharge and fresh makeup water consumption, saving raw water treatment costs.
