1. How to Use the Calculator

This calculator evaluates cooling tower performance for power plants, HVAC systems, and industrial processing units. Follow these 4 simple steps:

1

Enter Temperature Readings

Input Hot Water Inlet Temp (Thot), Cold Water Outlet Temp (Tcold), and Ambient Wet-Bulb Temp (Twb).

2

Input Circulating Water Flow

3

Set Cycles of Concentration (CoC)

4

2. Description of Output Data: Where Each Property Is Used in Power Plants

The calculator evaluates 8 core cooling tower metrics. Below is an explanation of where each metric is applied in plant operations:

Output Property Symbol Engineering Application & Use Case in Power Plants
Cooling Tower Range ΔT Temperature difference between hot entering water and cold leaving water. Higher Range indicates higher thermal heat load extracted from steam turbine condensers.
Approach Temperature Approach Difference between cold leaving water and ambient wet-bulb temperature (Tcold - Twb). Indicates thermal adequacy (standard industrial approach is 3°C to 5°C).
Cooling Effectiveness η (%) Percentage ratio of actual cooling range achieved versus theoretical maximum potential cooling.
Cycles of Concentration CoC Ratio of dissolved mineral concentration in circulating basin water compared to raw makeup water. Higher CoC saves makeup water.
Evaporation Loss E Pure water vaporized into air to remove heat. Evaluates raw water consumption (~0.15% per 1°C range).
Blowdown Rate B Required water discharge rate to bleed off concentrated minerals, preventing tube scaling and silica precipitation.
Makeup Water Demand M Total fresh water supply rate required to balance Evaporation, Blowdown, and Drift losses.
Total Heat Rejection Qheat Total thermal energy rejected by the cooling tower to atmosphere, expressed in MWth, Gcal/hr, or TR.

3. Description of Uncommon Engineering Units Supported in This Calculator

Different industrial sectors and regions use specialized thermal units. Below are 4 units supported by this tool:

TR (Refrigeration Tons)

Tons of Refrigeration

Where Used: HVAC, chiller plant, and industrial process cooling towers. 1 TR = 3.517 kW = 3024 kcal/hr.

Gcal/hr

Gigacalories per Hour

Where Used: Thermal power plant condenser duty and district heating balances in Asia and Europe. 1 Gcal/hr = 1.163 MWth.

GPM (US)

Gallons per Minute

Where Used: Standard volumetric flow unit in North American power plants and cooling towers. 1 GPM = 0.2271 m³/hr.

TPH (Tons/hr)

Metric Tons per Hour

Where Used: Mass flow rate unit used in boiler feedwater and cooling tower circulating pump logs. 1 TPH = 1 m³/hr water.

4. Water Loss & Performance Calculation Formulas

Understanding the mathematical derivations helps plant engineers optimize blowdown discharge and chemical dosing:

Cooling Effectiveness (%) Formula

η =
Thot - Tcold Thot - Twb
× 100%

Evaporation Loss (E) Formula

E = 0.00085 × Q × (Thot - Tcold)

Approximately 0.15% of circulating flow evaporates per 1°C of cooling range achieved.

Blowdown Rate (B) Formula

B =
Evaporation Loss (E) CoC - 1

Blowdown prevents scale accumulation by bleeding off concentrated dissolved minerals.

Total Makeup Water Demand (M)

M = Evaporation (E) + Blowdown (B) + Drift Loss

Maintains constant water level in the cooling tower cold water basin.

5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What is the difference between Cooling Tower Range and Approach?

Range is the temperature difference between hot entering water and cold leaving water (Thot - Tcold). Approach is the difference between cold leaving water temperature and ambient wet-bulb temperature (Tcold - Twb).

Q: Can a cooling tower cool water below ambient wet-bulb temperature?

No. Ambient wet-bulb temperature (Twb) is the theoretical thermodynamic limit for evaporative cooling. A cooling tower can approach wet-bulb temperature (typically within 3°C to 5°C) but can never cool water below Twb.

Q: Why is increasing Cycles of Concentration (CoC) important?

Increasing CoC from 2.0 to 4.0 or 5.0 drastically reduces required blowdown discharge and fresh makeup water consumption, saving raw water treatment costs and chemical dosing expenses.

Q: How does ambient humidity affect cooling tower performance?

High ambient humidity raises wet-bulb temperature (Twb), reducing the cooling tower's evaporative potential. On hot, humid days, cooling tower approach increases, resulting in warmer cold water returning to the steam turbine condenser.

Q: How is total heat rejection (Qheat) calculated?

Total heat rejection equals mass flow rate multiplied by specific heat capacity and temperature range: Q = ṁ × Cp × (Thot - Tcold). Expressed in MW (thermal), Gcal/hr, or TR.
